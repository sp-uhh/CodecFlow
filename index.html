CodecFlow: Efficient Fullband Speech Restoration via Flow Matching in a Neural Codec Latent Space

Huajian Fang, Simon Welker, Timo Gerkmann

Abstract

Generative models have made significant progress in speech restoration tasks. Although most generative pipelines operate in the spectral domain, recent advances in neural codecs have sparked interest in operating in latent spaces. In this work, we focus on a residual vector-quantized latent space, in which the codebook is a finite set of feature vectors embedded in a continuous vector space. Most existing studies typically model them as discrete variables; however, how to exploit the continuous geometric properties of embedding vectors to achieve generative speech restoration remains underexplored. For this, we explore conditional flow matching as a generative modeling approach in the codec's quantized latent space for the tasks of speech enhancement, speech dereverberation, and bandwidth extension. We compare against a predictive model that shares the same architecture and is trained in the same latent space to demonstrate the contribution of generative modeling, and against a spectral-domain diffusion method to investigate modeling in the latent space. We reveal that operating in the latent space can reduce the computational cost of generative speech restoration by two orders of magnitude. We further analyze where the generative formulation is beneficial across the three tasks.
